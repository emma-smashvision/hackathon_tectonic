# UX critique — round 2

## Q1. Keep one optional Drive view; exclude it from the five-minute script

The [game proposal](proposal-game.md) makes a useful distinction: driving can reveal shared experience without advancing personal life. Keep that idea. My first proposal already allowed optional guided 3D; I do not support restoring the asset city.

“Drive this road” opens the selected life road at the selected stop. Show one avenue, month posts, aggregate ghost riders and the next surprise. Keep the road title, actual-position label and “Exploring month…” visible. Tap “Next stop” for automatic travel; assisted steering is optional. Open the same evidence sheet and return to the same 2D position. No possessions district, readiness scores, forced introduction or exit debrief. Care roads stay in the quiet 2D experience.

Do not allocate core pitch time to driving. The juror-note roundtrip and shared evidence across channels demonstrate the unique technology. Keep Drive ready for questions after the pitch. Ship its entry only after road/stop parity works; an unfinished city must not delay the accessible flow.

## Q2. Everyday plus one confirmed life road on day one

Adopt the [pitch proposal’s](proposal-pitch.md) day-one limit. Several concurrent life roads need deduplication, separate positions and clear removal controls. Design for them, but do not imply they already work.

Tom first sees “Moving house · before your move”, “You told us: 1 November”, and a labelled You marker. Below: the next crowd surprise, what helped and Read notes. Two explicit switches read **Moving house** and **Everyday · October**. **All roads** stays visible. The selected life road takes priority over routine bills; the timeline runs downward into the future.

Explored roads sit under **Exploring**, never **On now**. Confirming another life road requires an explicit switch; browsing never replaces Tom’s move. State the day-one limitation plainly.

## Q3. One crowd-led Home card; nudges open its stop

Inside the v1 bubbles Home, use one readable card: **Moving house — before 1 November**; “Small setup costs surprised earlier movers”; **See what helped**. Add a small “You told us” link and synthetic-data label. Its text and statistics come from the selected road’s evidence, not marketing copy.

Tap opens that exact stop sheet, with the road name, position and Back to road visible. A nudge opens the same sheet after unlock; its lock-screen text contains no situation or amount. Urgent banking protection retains priority. Nadia gets an adviser invitation, not an unsolicited crowd nudge.

## Q4. Find a road first; Kate uses the same catalogue matcher

Use **Find a road** in All roads, accepting ordinary phrases. Return named catalogue matches; let the customer confirm the road and stage. Kate can invoke the same flow.

Matching returns only validated road IDs. The renderer obtains all numbers, notes and stop labels from published catalogue evidence through the crowd gate. Generated prose cannot supply statistics. Unknown situations say “No published road yet”; ambiguous ones offer choices. Keep browsing separate from placement. This works with a local synonym table and remains usable without a model response.

## Q5. Steal and veto

**Steal:** the [crowd proposal’s](proposal-crowd.md) warning about behaviour bars. “People with good outcomes often did X” does not mean “Doing X causes good outcomes”. Preserve that distinction in labels and ranking.

**Veto:** the pitch proposal’s eight-hour commitment for navigation, evidence repairs, consent, notes and six-persona verification. Reduce scope openly; do not fund the spectacle by skipping trust or accessibility checks.
