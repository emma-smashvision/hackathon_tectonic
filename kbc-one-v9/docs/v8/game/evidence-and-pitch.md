# KBC One v8 — Evidence, benchmarks and pitch

**Recommendation:** make each stop an optional learning moment: guess → reveal → explain → prepare. Driving supplies the setting; understanding supplies the value.

Design/research only, 18 October 2026. Sources below were verified online. KBC-specific benefits remain hypotheses.

## 1. What the evidence supports

**Strongest support: active thought and recall, within studied settings.**

- **Active learning:** Freeman et al. (2014), [“Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics,” *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111), reviewed 225 studies. Active learning improved undergraduate STEM performance. This supports participation over passive exposure; it does not establish that excitement, driving, or banking games cause learning.
- **Testing effect:** Roediger and Karpicke (2006), [“Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention,” *Psychological Science*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/), found better delayed recall after tests than restudy, including after 1 week. Immediate performance favoured restudy. Agarwal, Nunes and Blunt (2021), [“Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning: a Systematic Review of Applied Research in Schools and Classrooms,” *Educational Psychology Review*](https://remix.berklee.edu/faculty-works/12/), found benefits across 50 classroom experiments. For KBC, later recall of a preparation step fits this mechanism better than remembering a score.

**Promising, with narrower or mixed evidence.**

- **Guess before reveal:** Kornell, Hays and Bjork (2009), [“Unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance subsequent learning,” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015729), found that failed attempts followed by the answer could improve subsequent learning. Guessing an unseen crowd result is **pretesting**, not classic retrieval of previously learned information. Apply this as a hypothesis: invite a low-pressure guess, promptly reveal the answer and explain it. Do not leave an incorrect answer uncorrected.
- **Serious games:** Wouters, van Nimwegen, van Oostendorp and van der Spek (2013), [“A meta-analysis of the cognitive and motivational effects of serious games,” *Journal of Educational Psychology*](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-cognitive-and-motivational-effects-of-seri/), found learning and retention benefits, but no statistically significant motivation advantage. Games with additional instruction performed better. A drive without explanation is not an evidence-based lesson.
- **Gamification is mixed:** Sailer and Homner (2020), [“The Gamification of Learning: a Meta-analysis,” *Educational Psychology Review*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), found positive average effects. Cognitive effects survived stricter study-quality analysis; motivational and behavioural effects were less stable. Context and design varied. Points, badges and leaderboards are not universal learning tools.
- **Finance-specific evidence:** Cannistrà, De Beckker, Agasisti, Amagir, Põder, Vartiak and De Witte (2024), [“The impact of an online game-based financial education course: Multi-country experimental evidence,” *Journal of Comparative Economics*](https://research.hva.nl/en/publications/the-impact-of-an-online-game-based-financial-education-course-mul/), reported a 0.313-standard-deviation literacy gain in a randomized trial with 2,220 students across 4 countries. This supports the tested course, not an isolated game mechanic, adult banking outcomes, or lasting financial wellbeing.

**Evidence gap:** this review verifies no causal study showing that KBC-style crowd roads improve adult preparation or reduce adviser workload. Do not present that claim as established.

## 2. Real benchmarks: success, tradeoffs and limits

| Benchmark | Verified result | Lesson for KBC |
|---|---|---|
| **Duolingo** | Its [company experiment report](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/) says separating streaks from daily goals increased day-14 retention by 3.3% relative. Fewer learners reached daily goals. | Return visits and learning effort can diverge. This is company-reported product evidence, not proof of knowledge retention. Borrow short participation; omit streak pressure. |
| **BBVA Game** | BBVA [reported](https://www.bbva.com/en/innovation/bbva-receives-bank-innovation-award-gamification-platform-bbva-game/) over 100,000 players in its first 6 months, with points for transactions and rewards. | Adoption is verified as a company claim. Learning gains and customer harm are unverified here. Do not copy incentives to transact or claim KBC is the first gamified bank. |
| **NatWest Island Saver** | NatWest [describes](https://www.natwestgroup.com/news-and-insights/news-room/press-releases/our-updates/2021/oct/natwest-picks-up-kids-banking-fintech-roostermoney.html) a free console/mobile game that teaches young people money management. | A bank can offer playable education. This source does not establish efficacy or adult transfer; no verified backfire is claimed. |

**Demonstrated adverse direction:** Gathergood et al. (2024), [“Digital engagement practices: a trading apps experiment,” *FCA Research Note*](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/fca-research/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment), tested over 9,000 consumers. Engagement features could increase trading frequency and risk-taking. This was an experimental platform, not proof against every named app. It shows why engagement alone is a poor bank objective.

## 3. Jury-ready lines

1. “Research supports learning through participation and recall. We turn a financial information page into a question, an explanation and a useful next step.”
2. “Take a guess, see what earlier travellers experienced, then choose how to prepare. Today, the crowd is clearly labelled synthetic.”
3. “The crowd’s data becomes the lesson: what happened, what was associated with better outcomes, and what you can ask or do next.”
4. “We will measure what people remember a week later and whether they prepare—not how long they keep driving.”

**Trust line:** “A bank must make understanding rewarding, preserve the choice to skip, and never reward risk, spending or constant return visits.”

## 4. Measurement KBC could run

Randomize consenting adults on one non-care road, once per customer:

- **A:** the read-only stop sheet, with its explanation and action controls.
- **B:** an optional guess before the identical sheet, with immediate explanatory feedback and a visible **Show the answer** option.

Keep content, evidence version, action access and follow-up contact equal. Use 2D in both arms to isolate the question from driving. Record exposure time without forcing equal dwell time. Keep Large & simple on an equivalent non-game path; evaluate its comprehension separately.

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Primary: recall after 1 week** | Blind-score unaided recall of the preparation step and its reason, before showing the answer. Include one new scenario to test transfer. |
| **Preparation** | At 7/30 days, separate saved reminders/questions from completed actions. Label self-reported completion; verify only with consent. |
| **Adviser demand and preparation** | Compare relevant contacts per randomized customer over 30 days. Separately assess question clarity and documents prepared using a fixed adviser rubric. |
| **Guardrails** | Check trust, anxiety, abandonment, accessibility, unresolved needs, and mistaken belief that crowd results predict personal outcomes. |

Pre-register the primary outcome, minimum useful improvement, sample size, follow-up window and safety thresholds. Analyse by assignment, including skippers. Report confidence intervals and missing follow-ups by arm. Do not cherry-pick completers. Fewer calls count as improvement only alongside resolved needs and maintained access; better-prepared calls can be success even without fewer calls. Minimize stored data; never use quiz answers for credit or sales profiling.

## 5. Minimal demo and 1–1.5-day increment

Add one reviewed prompt to the existing `moving-house / energy-new-address` sheet: “What would you record at key handover to help check a later energy bill?” Offer **Meter readings**, **Not sure**, and **Show the answer**. Reveal the meter-photo explanation, one available gated crowd association, and the existing reminder action. The prompt teaches a practical concept; it does not ask users to memorize a synthetic percentage.

Spend 30–40 seconds inside the 5-minute demo: guess, reveal, save. At the close, ask for the preparation step without reopening the sheet. Record immediate comprehension and ability to explain “synthetic”; this cannot demonstrate week-long retention. Preserve the juror-note handoff. Optional Drive remains at most 15 seconds.

Scope: one prompt, feedback state, skip path, existing action and accessibility checks. Budget about 1 day plus half a day for verification and rehearsal; no new scoring system or backend. Use the [crowd adapter](../../../src/one/crowd/README.md), shared road/stop IDs and [v8 design](../v8-design.md).

## 6. Risks and forbidden claims

- Every crowd number and visual encoding must pass k ≥ 500; keep synthetic labels visible. Withheld evidence stays withheld. The gate alone does not prove production anonymity.
- The [engine](../../../src/hindsight/engine/simulate.ts) builds outcomes from simulation assumptions. Its associations cannot validate themselves. Never claim causation, actual KBC customer results, or individual prediction.
- “Surprised” cost rates model costs exceeding capacity; they are not surveyed feelings. Outcome-ranked help remains association-based and includes non-product actions.
- No gambling, loot boxes, real-money stakes, shaming, wealth rankings or dark-pattern streak pressure. Care roads never receive game prompts. Skipping preserves all information and help.
- Do not claim “games always teach better,” “excitement proves learning,” “all other banks only offer pages,” guaranteed savings, or proven call reduction. Synthetic notes are examples, not testimonials.
